# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Pagebound is for casual-to-mid-core players who enjoy survivors-like action roguelites, readable buildcraft, stylized hand-drawn worlds, pets, collections, and repeatable 30-minute runs. Primary players are on PC / Steam, with Steam Deck support as a later optimization target. They want low-control-complexity combat that still rewards positioning, dash timing, routing, upgrade choices, and build planning.

Players come to Pagebound for approachable chaos: they want the page to fill with color, marks, pickups, and damage numbers while the underlying information remains legible. They are turned away by weak-feeling early combat, bland empty arenas, unreadable late-game effects, imprecise controls, and pets or upgrades that feel cosmetic.

## Product Purpose

Pagebound is a 2.5D storybook action roguelite where tiny hand-drawn heroes fight living scribbles, blankness, and torn-page nightmares by redrawing a magical page with color, Waxlight, stickers, dream trails, pets, and huge damage numbers.

Its purpose is to make the player feel that the story is almost gone, but they are strong enough to redraw the ending. The product succeeds when players understand the core loop quickly, feel powerful early, chase Page Events because the rewards matter, and read the battlefield even when the screen becomes visually intense.

The core differentiator is Pagecraft: the finite battlefield is a living physical page. Attacks leave marks, dashes activate or reshape those marks, enemies corrupt space, and Page Events force movement across the page.

## Brand Personality

Pagebound should feel cute, magical, tactile, readable, and absurdly powerful.

The tone is childlike wonder with strong mechanical confidence. It should feel like a hand-drawn storybook kingdom came alive under real light, not like a generic fantasy parchment interface. The game can be whimsical, but the UI and feedback must remain disciplined because players make fast combat decisions under pressure.

## Anti-references

- School-supply roguelite identity: classrooms, homework, pencil cases, rulers, scissors, lunch trays, and school-coded UI as the main fantasy.
- Generic fantasy parchment HUD: beige panels, ornate frames, decorative scrollwork, and medieval UI that could belong to any RPG.
- Beige paper clutter: low-contrast tan-on-tan surfaces, busy paper textures behind critical text, and UI that disappears into the page.
- Unreadable late-game soup: effects, damage numbers, pets, and event markers competing without a clear priority order.
- Weak or cosmetic pets: companions that are minor stat sticks, invisible helpers, or emotional decoration without clear gameplay value.
- Generic survivors clone presentation: flat arena logic, anonymous enemy swarms, and upgrade cards that do not express Pagecraft or storybook material identity.

## Design Principles

1. **The Page Is Alive.** If a feature could happen on any generic arena, it needs a page-state interaction, a material consequence, or a visible mark.
2. **Power First, Clarity Always.** Players should feel strong, but player danger, boss telegraphs, Page Event objectives, and pickup feedback must stay readable.
3. **Childlike Fantasy, Not Classroom Props.** The page is the medium. The fantasy is crowns, pets, moons, stars, clouds, forests, toy-like keepsakes, and storybook kingdoms.
4. **Every Reward Must Explain Its Value.** Draft cards, Page Event rewards, pet upgrades, and pickups must make their gameplay effect obvious at speed.
5. **Pets Must Be Felt.** Pets should create visible support or combat value that changes how the run feels. They must not vanish into passive math.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA intent for player-facing UI where practical. Prioritize high contrast over textured backgrounds, readable text sizes, non-color-only state cues, and HUD scaling.

Damage numbers are part of the fantasy, but they need accessibility modes: full, reduced, boss/crit only, off, scale controls, and high-contrast treatment. Motion should support reduced-motion settings. Important combat information should not depend on hue alone; shape, placement, iconography, and motion should reinforce color.
